Show a revision in plain text

The strikethrough text generator adds a line or slash mark to individual characters. Strikethrough text for corrections still contains the original letters, so pair a crossed-out word with plain replacement text when the change needs to be clear.

Choose a line that makes the edit clear

Strikethrough can suggest a correction, a joke, or a visible change of mind. Chic Strikes adds a horizontal line over bold-looking letters. Classic Slashes and Chic Slashes use diagonal marks. Slippy and Slippy Vintage give the line another angle and weight. Enter the exact word into the strikethrough text generator and compare whether a reader can still identify the original. The best crossed-out result shows both the old wording and the fact that it changed.

Strikethrough text for corrections should make the replacement easy to find. In a phrase such as old plan followed by new plan, generate the old words only and type the new words plainly beside them. Crossing out both sides removes the contrast. A diagonal slash may feel playful, while a straight line can read more like an edit. Choose the tone that matches the message, then check that the corrected wording is unmistakable.

  • Use a straight line for a clear edit.
  • Try a slash for a more playful aside.
  • Keep the replacement word unmarked.

Make a revision understandable without context

A crossed-out joke works when readers can infer the intended second thought. Write the full sentence in plain text first, then decide which word should visibly change. For a real correction, include the correct value in normal letters immediately after the marked value. Do not rely on the line alone to tell someone which date, price, or instruction is current. The original characters remain present and may still be copied or read.

The strikethrough text generator is handy for short phrases because you can compare several line treatments quickly. It is less useful for a long revision history. A heavily marked paragraph can become hard to scan, and readers may not know whether an old detail is still valid. For important updates, write a clear sentence explaining what changed, then use strikethrough only as an optional visual reference.

  • State the corrected value plainly.
  • Cross out only the superseded word or short phrase.
  • Explain important changes in normal text.

Understand what the copied line contains

This effect is made by adding a mark to individual characters. It is different from selecting text in an editor and pressing a formatting button. Spaces may not carry a visible line, so a multiword result can look broken between words. Numbers and punctuation can also show marks at slightly different positions. Enter a realistic example with all the characters you intend to use and inspect it before copying.

Strikethrough text for corrections is still text. The old letters do not disappear, and someone may be able to copy them or encounter them through assistive reading. If the original value must not be seen, remove it entirely instead of crossing it out. If the mark makes a digit ambiguous, write the corrected information in plain text. The visual effect is useful for showing a change, but it is not a way to hide information.

  • Check spaces in multiword phrases.
  • Inspect dates and numbers carefully.
  • Remove sensitive old text rather than styling it.

Inspect the line in the receiving app

Paste a candidate into a draft and look closely at the line. The position of each mark depends on the font chosen by the destination. A slash may sit through the center of one letter but cover another almost completely. A horizontal line may appear uneven where letters have very different heights. Try more than one preview if the crossed-out word is hard to decipher, and check the result on a narrow screen when possible.

If the receiving editor offers a built-in strikethrough button, compare that result too. Editor formatting can be easier to maintain when you need to revise a long note later. The copyable Unicode version is useful when a field has no such control, but it can add extra characters that affect limits or search. Keep your source phrase so you can regenerate a clean result instead of repairing individual marks in a pasted line.

  • Check line position over letters and numbers.
  • Compare the destination formatting control when available.
  • Regenerate after a wording change.

Avoid making the correction harder to read

A reader should not have to decode the marked word to understand the final message. Put the correct statement next to it, with enough plain spacing or punctuation to separate the two. For example, a playful changed plan can work in a casual message; a meeting time needs a direct statement of the final time. Strikethrough is a visual cue, not a replacement for clear revision wording.

If a line looks crowded, switch to a lighter strike or skip the effect. Some readers and assistive tools may interpret added marks inconsistently, and searching for the old word may behave differently from plain text. The strikethrough text generator is best used for a short, optional accent where the message still makes sense without the line. That keeps the humor or edit visible without making the current information uncertain.

  • Put the final wording in ordinary text.
  • Do not use a crossed-out line as the only update notice.
  • Choose clarity when marks obscure the letters.

Strikethrough text questions

Does strikethrough delete the original words?

No. The original characters remain in the text with visible marks added to them.

Can I cross out just one word in a sentence?

Generate and copy that word, then place it beside ordinary text in your destination.
